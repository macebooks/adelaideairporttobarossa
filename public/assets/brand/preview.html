Brand concept · adelaideairporttobarossa.com.au

The Last Leg

One continuous journey, drawn in one line: a gold runway light, a dotted road that lifts off the tarmac, and a grape cluster where it lands. Departure is the airport; arrival is the valley. The mark tells the whole service in a glance. The name is the domain itself — it says the route and the search query — so the mark and type treatment supply the character the domain can’t.

02 · What the mark says
Anatomyone line, four beats
  1. The runway light. A single gold point marks departure — the tarmac at ADL. It is also the brand's accent colour doing its one job.
  2. The dotted road. The same dashed line that runs through the whole site — the Sturt Highway, rising as it leaves the airport behind.
  3. The turn to green. The last dash changes colour and becomes a vine stem: the exact moment the trip stops being travel and starts being holiday.
  4. The cluster. Three grapes, the destination. Wine-dark, drawn from the same circles as the current site mark, so nothing on the page feels replaced — only sharpened.

05 · Usage

Do

  • Set the wordmark in Bricolage Grotesque 800, lowercase, broken as adelaideairport / tobarossa.com.au — with “to” in wine on light grounds, gold on dark. Tagline in Space Mono caps.
  • Use the reverse cut on ink, vine and wine grounds; mono ink for single-colour print.
  • Keep clear space around the mark of at least the height of one grape.
  • Wire the favicon with <link rel="icon" href="assets/brand/favicon.svg" type="image/svg+xml">.

Don't

  • Don't recolour the grapes outside wine (light grounds) or gold (dark grounds).
  • Don't use the full mark below 48 px — switch to the favicon cut.
  • Don't outline the grapes with heavy strokes at small sizes; the flat cut reads cleaner.
  • Don't rotate the mark; the road must always rise left to right — that's the story.
